Clubhouse

Privacy

How Clubhouse handles your data

Clubhouse runs entirely in your web browser. There is no Clubhouse server, so nobody else (including whoever runs this site) can see your email or your scores.

What it reads

When you connect Gmail or Outlook, Clubhouse searches your mailbox for golf words such as “tee time” and “stableford”, then reads those messages on your device to pick out tee times, competitions, handicap updates, club news and golf orders. Messages that turn out not to be about golf are skipped and not kept.

Access is read-only. Clubhouse can’t send, delete, move or mark email.

What it keeps

  • For each golf email: the sender, subject, date, a short preview and the details picked out of it, such as the date, time, course and booking reference.
  • The scores and tee times you enter, your settings, and the names of your playing partners with any photos you choose for them (made small first).

All of this is stored in your browser on this device.

Backup

If backup is switched on (it is unless you turn it off in Settings), a copy of the above goes to a private Clubhouse folder in your own Google Drive or OneDrive. That lets your scores follow you to another device. Clubhouse can only reach its own folder, not the rest of your files.

Weather

For tee times in the next two weeks, Clubhouse shows the forecast. It looks the course up by name on OpenStreetMap, remembers where it is on this device, and asks Open-Meteo for the forecast there. Only the course name and its position are sent, nothing about you. You can turn this off in Settings, under Home screen.

Distances on the course

If you turn on distances, Clubhouse uses the phone’s GPS while you score hole by hole. Your position is used on the phone to work out the distance to the green and is never sent anywhere. To find a course’s greens, Clubhouse asks OpenStreetMap (through its Overpass service) for the course at that spot, once, and keeps the answer with your scores. Greens you save yourself are kept the same way.

Sign-in

You sign in on Google’s or Microsoft’s own page; Clubhouse never sees your password. The access it gets back is kept in memory and only ever sent to Google or Microsoft. Google’s access lasts up to an hour. Microsoft’s can be renewed until you close the tab, for a day at most.

What it doesn’t do

  • No adverts, analytics or tracking of any kind.
  • Nothing is sold or shared. Your data goes nowhere except your browser and, for backup, your own Drive or OneDrive. (The weather lookup above sends a course name, not your data.)
  • Fonts and icons come from this site, so no other company is told when you open it.

Clubhouse’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

  • Disconnect (Settings) removes the golf emails from the device and cancels Clubhouse’s access, or shows you where to do that in your Google or Microsoft account.
  • Delete everything (Settings) removes all Clubhouse data from the device.
  • To remove the backup: in Google Drive, open Settings, then Manage apps, then Clubhouse’s Options, then “Delete hidden app data”. In OneDrive, delete the Apps › Clubhouse folder.

Questions

Clubhouse is open source. You can read exactly what it does, or ask a question, at github.com/OpenNerdz/clubhouse.
